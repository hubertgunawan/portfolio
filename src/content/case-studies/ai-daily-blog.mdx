## Problem

Writing consistently is hard to sustain alongside other work. I wanted a blog about AI engineering and developer tools that publishes on a schedule, and I wanted to treat it as a real production system: automated, observable, and safe to leave running.

## My role

Sole engineer. I designed the agent pipeline, the publishing flow, and the site at [blog.haheng.au](https://blog.haheng.au).

## Agent design

- **Separate roles.** Research, writing, and review are separate steps, so each prompt stays focused and each output can be checked on its own.
- **Structured hand-offs.** Each step passes structured output to the next instead of free text, which makes failures easier to detect.
- **Bilingual output.** Posts are published in English and Indonesian.

## Key decisions

- **Treat it like a service, not a script.** Scheduled runs, logs, and clear failure states, so a bad run doesn't silently publish.
- **Guardrails before publish.** A review step sits between generation and publishing.
- **Static output.** Posts are published as static pages, so the site stays fast and cheap to host.

## Limitations & safety

- LLMs can state things confidently that are wrong. Posts are framed around sources and practical takeaways, and the review step is there to catch obvious errors.
- Automated content needs a kill switch. Runs can be paused without touching the published site.

## Tech stack

AI agents · LLM APIs · Next.js · Scheduled jobs · RSS

## Lessons

- Most of the engineering in an agent system is around the model: scheduling, validation, retries, and deciding when *not* to publish.
- Small, single-purpose agent steps are much easier to debug than one large prompt.
