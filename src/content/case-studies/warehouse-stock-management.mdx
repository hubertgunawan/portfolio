## Problem

Warehouse stock was tracked manually. Counts were written down and later re-entered into other systems, which made stock levels slow to update and easy to get wrong. Operations staff needed a way to record stock movements on the warehouse floor, as they happened.

## Key decisions

- **API-first backend.** A RESTful API in Node.js served both the handheld app and the web tools, so business rules lived in one place.
- **ORM-based data layer.** Modelling stock, locations, and movements through an ORM kept the schema explicit and migrations reviewable.
- **Containers from day one.** Docker images and Kubernetes manifests made environments reproducible and deployments repeatable.
- **Tests before release.** Unit tests and test automation caught regressions before they reached the warehouse floor.

## Outcome

The handheld app replaced manual inventory tracking for warehouse operations staff, so stock movements were captured at the point of work instead of being re-keyed later.

## Lessons

- Floor tools live or die on speed: every extra tap is friction for staff who are carrying stock.
- Putting validation in the API, not just the client, kept data clean across every app that used it.
